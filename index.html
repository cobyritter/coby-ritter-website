About Me

As a freshman at the University of Wisconsin-Madison, I am excited to pursue a career in investment banking. My passion for learning, my teachability, and my work ethic are the foundation of how I approach both academics and life. At a young age, I developed a fascination with understanding complex systems and how different parts work together to create something much larger. That curiosity began with cars and military aviation, and it eventually moved into economics, where I became fascinated by how shifts in consumer confidence affect aggregate demand and GDP.

Growing up in a family full of engineers, I spent a lot of time listening to my grandfather, who worked as a naval engineer, and my father, who works in traffic safety, talk about how systems interact, how problems are solved, and why every detail matters. That mindset carried into business and finance, where I began to see the same curiosity applied to markets, businesses, and major financial decisions. My experiences in competitive lacrosse and BBYO taught me how to work under pressure, communicate effectively, and support a team. Those experiences, combined with my interest in finance, led me to pursue investment banking, a field where I can continue learning, competing, and helping businesses make strategic, impactful decisions.

Why Investment Banking

01

Global Impact

I am drawn to work that creates meaningful impact, and investment banking is a place where that impact is amplified on a much larger scale. Through leadership in community service initiatives, I helped organize food drives, sports equipment drives, and road cleanups, which reinforced my belief that strong decisions can create value for people beyond the immediate moment. I want to apply that same mindset in banking, where the work influences growth, capital allocation, and the long-term success of businesses and communities.

02

Steep Learning Curve

Coming to Madison pushed me into a more demanding academic environment, with harder classes and a faster pace than I had experienced before. I learned to adjust quickly, stay organized, and absorb information at a higher level. I work best in environments that are intense, fast-moving, and intellectually demanding, because they force me to learn faster, think deeper, and improve continuously.

03

Community of Driven People

One of the biggest reasons I am excited about this path is the environment itself. At Madison, I have become more intentional about getting involved in classes and clubs that challenge me and push me to learn. Being surrounded by driven peers has made me more motivated, more curious, and more eager to improve. The clubs and communities I have joined have helped shape how I approach learning, teamwork, and ambition.
